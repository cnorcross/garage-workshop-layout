Build notes

Miter saw station

  • 8 feet long against the left wall, saw dropped into the middle so its table sits flush with both wings.
  • A sliding saw's rails travel backward. Measure how far yours slides and make the station deep enough (about 28″) that the rails clear the drywall.
  • Add a fence with a flip stop on the wings for repeat cuts, and a dust hood behind the blade tied to the shop vac.
  • Cabinets or drawers underneath replace the storage the kitchen table never gave you.

Traditional workbench

  • About 6′ × 22–24″ fits this garage. A Nicholson (English) bench or a split-top Roubo both work well for hand planes and are built from construction lumber.
  • Laminate the top from Douglas fir or southern yellow pine, 3″ or thicker for mass. Your cherry is a good choice for the vise chop.
  • Leg vise at the front-left end (a screw kit with a parallel guide), or a quick-release front vise if you want a simpler install.
  • Add a row of ¾″ dog holes, a pair of holdfasts and a planing stop. Height around your palm with arms hanging, often 33–35″ for planing.

Table saw and outfeed

  • Rip along the length of the garage toward the back wall. Open the garage door for anything longer than 8′.
  • Rebuild the 2x4basics kit smaller, about 30″ × 40″, as the outfeed and assembly table. Its legs set the top at about 36″, so measure your saw table first.
  • If the kit sits higher than the saw, keep it as the assembly table and add a fold-down outfeed support to the saw stand.

Drill press / grinder stand

  • Bolt each tool to its own plywood base cut to the same size, with cleats or a pair of toggle clamps holding it to the stand top. A swap then takes a minute and the drill press goes back in the same spot.
  • Grinder sparks plus sawdust is a fire risk. Sweep the area first, aim the sparks at the floor rather than toward the outfeed table, and never vacuum up hot grit with the shop vac.
  • For plane irons, use the grinder only to reshape a bevel or remove a chip. Grind lightly and dip often so the edge never turns blue, then hone by hand.

Dust collection on a shop vac

  • Put a cyclone separator (Dust Deputy or Dustopper style) ahead of the vac so the filter stays clean.
  • A tool-activated switch starts the vac when the saw starts.
  • Use a HEPA filter plus bags. Miter saws throw dust no hood fully catches, so wear a respirator for fine dust and consider a box-fan air cleaner with MERV 13 filters.

Power and light

  • A jobsite saw can pull close to 15 A on its own, so the saw and the vac on one 15 A circuit will trip the breaker. Map which outlets share a circuit and put the vac on a different one.
  • Bright 4000–5000K LED shop lights overhead, plus a light at the bench you can move low for raking light while planing.

Mounting on drywall

  • Screw French cleats, the lumber rack and wall cabinets into studs, usually 16″ on center. Drywall anchors won't carry clamps or lumber.
  • When the garage door is open it sits on ceiling tracks over roughly the front 8–9′ (the shaded zone on the plan). Keep the lumber rack and anything stacked on it below the tracks, and put any ceiling storage behind that zone.
  • If the wall you share with the house is fire-rated drywall, keep it intact: no cutouts.
